Lechon Kawali

Prep: 20 minsCook: 1 hr 30 minsServes: 6
View recipe

Ingredients

  • 1 kg pork belly (liempo), in 2 thick slabs
  • 1 tbsp rock salt
  • 1 tsp whole peppercorns
  • 3 dried bay leaves
  • 1 head garlic, halved crosswise
  • 4 cups cooking oil for deep-frying
  • Liver sauce or spiced vinegar for dipping

Steps

  1. Place pork in a pot with salt, peppercorns, bay leaves, garlic and enough water to cover
  2. Bring to a boil then simmer on medium-low for 1 hour until a fork slides in easily
  3. Drain and pat the skin very dry with paper towels. Rest uncovered for 30 minutes or refrigerate overnight for the crispiest skin
  4. Heat oil to about 180°C. Carefully lower pork in skin side down and cover with a splatter screen because it will pop
  5. Fry 10 to 15 minutes until the skin bubbles and turns golden and crisp
  6. Drain on a rack for 5 minutes then chop into bite-size pieces. Tip: dry skin is the secret to crackling

Sisig

Prep: 30 minsCook: 40 minsServes: 6
View recipe

Ingredients

  • 500 g pork belly
  • 200 g pork liver or ears (optional)
  • 2 medium red onions, finely chopped
  • 4 siling labuyo or 2 siling haba, chopped
  • 3 tbsp calamansi juice
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • Salt and ground black pepper
  • 1 tbsp butter or oil

Steps

  1. Boil pork belly in salted water for 30 minutes until tender then drain
  2. Grill or pan-fry the pork until the skin is crisp and browned on all sides
  3. Chop pork and liver into tiny pieces about the size of a peppercorn
  4. Heat butter in a pan then sauté half the onions for 2 minutes
  5. Add chopped pork, soy sauce, chilies and pepper and stir over high heat for 5 minutes
  6. Finish with calamansi juice and the remaining raw onions. Serve sizzling on a hot plate topped with an egg if desired

Pork Adobo

Prep: 15 minsCook: 45 minsServes: 6
View recipe

Ingredients

  • 1 kg pork belly, cut into 2-inch cubes
  • 1/2 cup soy sauce
  • 1/3 cup white or cane vinegar
  • 1 whole head garlic, crushed and peeled
  • 3 dried bay leaves
  • 1 tsp whole peppercorns
  • 1 cup water
  • 1 tbsp cooking oil

Steps

  1. Marinate pork in soy sauce and half of the garlic for 30 minutes
  2. Heat oil in a pot and sear pork on all sides until lightly browned
  3. Add the marinade, water, bay leaves, peppercorns and remaining garlic and bring to a boil
  4. Pour in the vinegar without stirring and let it boil for 3 minutes so the sharp smell cooks off
  5. Lower heat and simmer covered for 35 to 40 minutes until fork-tender
  6. Uncover and reduce the sauce until slightly thick and glossy. Tip: adobo tastes even better the next day

  • 2 carrots, diced

Crispy Pata

Prep: 30 minsCook: 2 hrsServes: 6

Ingredients

  • 1 whole pork hock (about 1.2 kg)
  • 1 head garlic, halved
  • 2 tbsp rock salt
  • 1 tbsp peppercorns
  • 4 bay leaves
  • 1 onion, quartered
  • Oil for deep-frying
  • Soy sauce, vinegar and chilies for dip

Steps

  1. Boil the hock with garlic, onion, salt, peppercorns and bay leaves for 1 hour 30 minutes until tender but not falling apart
  2. Lift out carefully and dry the skin thoroughly with paper towels
  3. Refrigerate uncovered overnight so the skin dries out
  4. Heat oil to 180°C in a deep pot and lower the pata in carefully, covering with a lid to avoid splatter
  5. Fry 15 to 20 minutes turning once until blistered and crisp
  6. Chop and serve with the dip. Tip: never skip drying the skin

Meal management tips and lessons

Menu planning

Plan meals for seven days at once. Start by selecting protein sources, then add vegetables and starch. Do not use the same main ingredient for consecutive days to achieve balanced nutrition and varied menus. Check available pantry supplies first before finalizing your weekly menu.

Healthy eating

Build each meal with vegetables, a lean protein and a modest rice portion. Choose steaming, grilling and simmering more often than deep frying. Limit salty patis, toyo and bagoong.

Food selection and buying

Shop with a list. Buy vegetables in season, check fish for clear eyes and red gills, and meat for firm texture with no sour smell. Read labels for expiry dates.

Proper food storage

Keep the fridge at 4°C or below and the freezer at -18°C. Cool cooked food within 2 hours. Store raw meat on the lowest shelf. First in, first out: use older stock first.

Portion control

Use the plate method: half vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter rice. Serve from the kitchen, not the pot on the table, and use smaller plates.

Budget-friendly planning

Buy at the palengke in the early morning, choose local seasonal produce, use bones and heads for soups, and cook in batches. Set a weekly food budget and track it.

Food safety

Wash hands, separate raw and cooked foods, use different boards, and cook meat until no pink remains. Reheat leftovers until steaming hot, and only once.

Cutting food waste

Turn leftover rice into garlic fried rice or arroz caldo, vegetable trimmings into stock and overripe bananas into turon or banana bread.

Diet and nutrition advice

Go, Grow, Glow

Go foods (rice, root crops) give energy. Grow foods (fish, meat, eggs, beans) build the body. Glow foods (vegetables, fruits) protect health. Every meal should include all three.

Swap smart

  • Brown or mixed rice instead of all white rice
  • Sinigang or tinola instead of fried dishes
  • Fresh fruit instead of sweet drinks
  • Water or calamansi juice with less sugar

Balance for every client

  • Diabetes-friendly: fewer sweets, steady carbs
  • Heart-healthy: less salt and fat
  • Growing kids: more calcium and iron
  • Always confirm medical diets with a doctor or dietitian

Daily habits

Eat three regular meals, drink 8 glasses of water, eat at least 5 servings of vegetables and fruit, and keep sugary and salty snacks occasional.
